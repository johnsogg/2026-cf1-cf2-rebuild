> Callback to the previous section. Natural languages are messy/ambiguous;
> programming languages borrow the same vocabulary (syntax, semantics,
> idioms) but each concept gets stricter and more literal. Reuse the same
> subheadings as the previous section so the parallel is obvious. Goals:
>
> - Syntax: a PL's grammar is formal and enforced by a parser — there's no
>   "figure it out with a little extra brain power" the way there is with
>   the garbled English example. Invalid syntax just fails.
> - Semantics: same syntax, different meaning depending on context — e.g.
>   `+` means numeric addition or string concatenation depending on operand
>   types. Maybe also touch overloading/coercion.
> - Idioms: PL idioms are patterns "insiders" recognize but that aren't
>   derivable from syntax alone (callback to the JS `x || default` example
>   from earlier). Unlike natural-language idioms, breaking one doesn't
>   cause a parse error — just marks you as an outsider/beginner.
> - Domain jargon: libraries/frameworks/APIs and DSLs layer their own
>   vocabulary on top of the base language. Decide whether this is its own
>   heading here or folds into Idioms. Keep the label "Domain Jargon" but
>   use expansive language to make the analogy land — an API is opaque
>   until you've learned it, same as law or medicine are opaque to
>   outsiders; once fluent, it works for you, almost like magic. "Insiders
>   club" angle ties back to the Idioms section's baseball-idiom framing.
