# Building Mastery

Earlier I mentioned that the medium of code lets work as roughly or precisely as
you'd like. Quick 'sketches' to think through ideas. Prototypes to argue for or
against. Engineered software for production use. In order to use the full
spectrum of this medium as a vehicle for ideas, you need to build mastery.

I often draw a parallel between the arts of computer programming and music. You
can sit down at a piano and noodle around, work out a melody or chords. Or you
can play a complete piece. The medium, instrument, mechanics are the same. The
differences are in what you're trying to do, your frame of mind, the level of
refinement of the ideas. Of course, you need to learn to not only play the
piano, but also how music works.

The same is true with code. It takes practice, repetition, and struggle.

## Learn To Code In Zero Easy Steps

Programming is not easy, but it can be fun, rewarding, expressive, and powerful.
You might see books with obnoxious titles like "Learn to code in 8 days", and
they sell well because that is what people want! But it is never what people
get.

The approach taken in this book is to introduce topics one at a time, and get a
little practice working with it, technically and creatively, before moving on to
the next concept. And just like somebody doesn't learn to play Beethoven on
their first piano lesson, it will take time with code.

## Creativity Is The Purpose

Gaining mastery in code adds to your creative repetoire by unlocking avenues to
express, explore, and experiment with ideas. This isn't necessarily everybody's
purpose for learning how to program, but it is the perspective I take in this
book.

These first couple sections have been more philosophical than you might expect,
but I felt it was necessary to situate the topic for the AI era, and for the
intended audience.
